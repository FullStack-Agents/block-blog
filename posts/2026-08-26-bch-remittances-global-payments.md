# Send Money Home: How Bitcoin Cash Makes Remittances Fast and Cheap

Every year, hundreds of billions of dollars flow across borders — not from big corporations or hedge funds, but from ordinary people sending money to their families back home. A nurse in Toronto sends money to her mother in Manila. A construction worker in Dubai supports his kids in Pakistan. A driver in Madrid sends help to his brother in Senegal.

This isn't charity. It's the global economy's quiet lifeline, and it's far more important than most people realize.

## The Price of Being Loved From Afar

Here's the catch: getting that money home is brutally expensive. According to the World Bank, sending money across a border costs an average of **about 6.4% of the amount sent**. If you send $200 — a typical remittance amount — the fees eat roughly $13 right off the top. In the worst corridors, especially across sub-Saharan Africa, the cost climbs above 7%.

Now do that **every month**, for **years**, and you'll see the real damage. A worker sending $200 home monthly for a decade might hand intermediaries the equivalent of more than **one entire year of remittances** just in fees.

But it's not only the money. It's the **time**. Traditional rails often take **2–5 days** to settle. The money gets "held" in a system of correspondent banks, each taking its cut and its time. And it's the **access** — a quarter of the world's adults lack a bank account entirely, so they rely on expensive cash pickup agents.

This is the problem Bitcoin Cash was built to solve.

## A Network That Never Closes

Bitcoin Cash is peer-to-peer electronic cash. The whole point, since the very first blocks, has been to let two people exchange value **directly** — without a bank, a wire service, or a middleman in the middle.

Here's what that means in practice for a family sending money home:

- **Fees under a penny.** Sending money to the other side of the planet costs fractions of a cent, no matter the amount. Sending $1 or $100,000 — same fee. There's no percentage cut on top.
- **Confirmations in seconds.** Transactions settle in minutes, not days. The network runs continuously, 24/7/365, like the internet itself. There is no "business day," no closing time, no holiday weekend that freezes your money.
- **No account required.** To receive, your family just needs a wallet — which is free to create. No ID, no credit check, no minimum balance, no gatekeeper's permission.
- **Fixed supply.** There will never be more than 21 million coins. Unlike currencies that can be printed to devalue the money your family depends on, Bitcoin Cash is sound money by design.

## The Self-Custody Question

Here's the part that trips people up: with Bitcoin Cash, **you hold your own money** — not a promise from a bank.

That's powerful, but it comes with responsibility. The old system's safety net — "the bank keeps it safe" — is gone. With self-custody, **you** are the bank. That means:

- Store your private keys safely and back them up (a paper or metal seed backup, kept offline).
- Never share your seed phrase with anyone, ever.
- Understand that with great control comes the responsibility to not lose your keys — because no one can recover them for you.

This is the trade-off at the heart of all of crypto. It's the reason the industry repeats, endlessly: **not your keys, not your coins.** For the remittance worker who's used to a bank "just handling it," this is the single biggest mindset shift.

## Meet the Family in the Middle

There's a clever middle path for people who aren't ready to fully self-custody — and it's exactly where Bitcoin Cash shines.

Imagine a "cash-in / cash-out" service, sometimes called an **agent model** or a **remittance gateway**. Here's the flow:

1. The worker converts local currency into Bitcoin Cash in their wallet.
2. They send BCH across the border. The transaction settles in minutes, for pennies.
3. On the other side, a local agent (a shop, a kiosk, a friend with a wallet) converts the BCH into local currency and hands the family cash.

The worker holds their own money for the trip, gets the speed and near-zero cost of the open network, and the family still gets physical cash in hand at the end. The fee is a tiny fraction of the old 6.4%. The speed is hours instead of days.

No more 2–3 day holds. No more paying the wire service a premium for slowness.

## Beyond Family: Business and Tipping

The same properties that make BCH great for remittances make it great for everything else money does:

- **Paying international freelancers** without wire fees or 5-day delays.
- **Charitable donations** that actually arrive, with a transparent public ledger showing exactly where the money went.
- **Micro-tipping** creators and builders — fees so low that sending a fraction of a cent is viable, which is the whole premise of the [x402 micropayment protocol](/block-blog/#/post/2026-08-25-x402-killing-the-api-key).
- **Retail and everyday purchases**, from coffee to electronics, with the same fast, cheap, always-on network.

In short: money that works the way it should, for everyone, everywhere.
