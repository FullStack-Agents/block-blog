# Wallets and Self-Custody

To send and receive Bitcoin Cash, you need a **wallet**. But not all wallets are
created equal. The most important distinction is whether you control your own
private keys.

## What is a wallet?

A wallet is software (or hardware) that manages your private keys and lets you
create transactions. It doesn't "store" coins like a pocket — it stores the keys
that prove ownership of coins on the blockchain.

## Custodial vs. non-custodial

- **Custodial wallets** hold your keys for you (e.g. an exchange). Convenient,
  but you're trusting a third party — *"not your keys, not your coins."*
- **Non-custodial wallets** let you hold your own keys. You are fully in
  control — and fully responsible.

## Self-custody best practices

1. **Back up your seed phrase** — write it down on paper or metal, never in a
   cloud or screenshot.
2. **Store the backup offline** in a safe place.
3. **Use a hardware wallet** for larger amounts.
4. **Never share your seed phrase** with anyone.

Self-custody is a core value of Bitcoin Cash. It puts financial sovereignty back
in your hands.
